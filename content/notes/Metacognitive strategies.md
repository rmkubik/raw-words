# 10/31/26
I learned about this concept from [[The Psychology of Software Teams]]. The idea here is that metacognitive strategies are things you apply intentionally to do thinking. So, you have some innate cognitive or maybe just subconscious cognitive traits. These metacognitive traits are one level above that.

There are a few Cat, the author of the [[The Psychology of Software Teams]] talks about:
- Active learning – rubber ducking or enumerating all the context for a problem you're having on paper so that it helps you solve it or learn from the problem better
- Worked examples – instead of speculating on and on about how something works, figure out how to get into that problem space and figure out how it works for real. Look for the actual unexpected problems and validate your ideas quickly.
- Generating – there's this psychology notion of [[The generation effect]]. When you do work yourself to discover information you are more likely to learn from it than if you just find your answer without effort.
