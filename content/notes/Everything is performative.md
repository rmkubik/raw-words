# 11/14/26
I'm thinking about this topic again after getting annoyed at "performative male reading" in public and the like.

I read this quote from C.S. Lewis on a related subject:

> “Critics who treat ‘adult’ as a term of approval, instead of as a merely descriptive term, cannot be adult themselves. To be concerned about being grown up, to admire the grown up because it is grown up, to blush at the suspicion of being childish; these things are the marks of childhood and adolescence. And in childhood and adolescence they are, in moderation, healthy symptoms. Young things ought to want to grow. But to carry on into middle life or even into early manhood this concern about being adult is a mark of really arrested development. When I was ten, I read fairy tales in secret and would have been ashamed if I had been found doing so. Now that I am fifty I read them openly. When I became a man I put away childish things, including the fear of childishness and the desire to be very grown up.”

And it reminds me of [[The Case for Embracing Cringe]].

These quotes are about letting go of the worry that you shouldn't do things you enjoyed because you're worried about being judged. They say, do whatever you want. Don't worry about what anyone else thinks.

I like that framing. But, I feel like there's a corollary that falls out of this too.

I think that everything we do is performative. Choosing the clothes we wear for that day can say we care about what we look like or we don't care. Staying in instead of going out. Anything we do carries some level of communication with the people around us.

I think it is fine to think about what choosing to read kids books in public as an adult communicates to the people around you. There is a social norm there that you're breaking. Pretending it doesn't exist won't help you.

I think a lot of these comments are more about authenticity than performance. You can perform and be yourself. An authentic performance.

I do think it is generally good to be authentic where you can. Often, we're called to hide who we are for the comfort of others. Or for our own comfort on a different axis. A lot of these cases we're avoiding fear and if we try to be ourselves we'll realize that our or others' discomfort will be brief and mild.

I also think its perfectly reasonable to not be authentic. I'm not going to rant about capitalism at my place of employment. I'm not going to fight my barista over the LLM generated flyers they have posted.

My conclusion here is like... Yeah, it is performative to choose to read a book in public. It is performative to choose which book you want to read. What kind of reader do you want to be? But like, that's fine. 

Read in public because you like to read. Read in public because you feel productive. Read in public because it makes you feel smart. Read in public because you hate scrolling on your cell phone. Read in public because you can't focus at home. Read in public because you want to.

In the wise words of Rap Monster from BTS on their song Mic Drop ft. Steve Aoki:

> Live yo life, man. Good luck.
